# Causal Validation -- Does Attention Actually Lead Price?

Transfer entropy + permutation test (200 permutations, 4-bin quantile discretization) and Granger causality (lag=1) between each attention-derivative feature and next-day return, per asset. See src/eval/causal_validation.py's module docstring for the full method.

**85 total (asset, feature) pairs tested** -- with this many simultaneous hypothesis tests, ~4.2 'significant at nominal p<0.05' results are expected purely by chance, even if NO genuine lead-lag relationship exists anywhere in this data. The nominal (uncorrected) counts below are reported for transparency but are NOT the headline finding -- the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-corrected counts are.

| Method | Nominal p<0.05 | Survive BH-FDR (q<0.05) | Survive Bonferroni |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Transfer entropy (nonlinear) | 5/85 | **0**/85 | 0/85 |
| Granger causality (linear) | 15/85 | **8**/85 | 2/85 |

**Transfer entropy finds zero pairs that survive correction** -- no robust evidence of genuine NONLINEAR lead-lag from any attention-derivative feature to next-day return, anywhere in this basket, once multiple comparisons are accounted for. ## Cross-reference against what the RL policy actually attends to

`docs/attribution_report_all_assets.md`'s permutation importance shows
which features each trained PPO policy relies on most; this section asks
whether those same features show statistically robust causal lead-lag
(the FDR-corrected Granger survivors above), or whether the policy is
weighting features with no such evidence — a warning sign for overfitting
to noise correlated with the policy's own past actions/portfolio state
rather than genuine information.

**GME is the standout case.** `pageviews_velocity` is GME's **#2**
most-attributed feature (`docs/attribution_report_GME.md`: mean action
shift 0.380, second only to `pageviews_acceleration`) AND one of only 8
(asset, feature) pairs in the entire 85-pair sweep whose Granger causality
survives FDR correction (q=0.041). `search_interest` and
`search_interest_velocity` are also both attribution-relevant for GME
(ranked #9 and present in the top features) and both Granger-significant
after correction. **This is genuine convergence between two independent
methods** — permutation importance (does shuffling this feature change the
trained policy's behavior) and Granger causality (does this feature's past
statistically help predict future returns) — agreeing on GME specifically.
Combined with `docs/walk_forward_upgrade_validation.md`'s finding that GME
is the one asset where an architecture upgrade (LSTM, then the ensemble)
produced a large, real walk-forward improvement, this is the closest thing
in this entire project to positive evidence that attention genuinely
carries exploitable information for at least one asset, not just an
RL agent finding a profitable pattern in noise.

**Elsewhere, the picture is weaker.** DOGE and PEPE's top attribution
features (`search_interest_velocity` for both) show NO significant
transfer entropy or Granger causality after correction — the RL policies
for these two assets may be relying on features whose predictive value,
if any, doesn't survive this rigorous a test, even though (per the
original walk-forward report) DOGE's plain-PPO policy is walk-forward
profitable. This doesn't mean DOGE's result is fake — profitability and
statistically-provable-at-this-sample-size causality are different bars,
and 500ish rows is a small sample for detecting a real but modest effect
— but it does mean this project cannot currently claim direct causal
proof for DOGE the way it arguably can, tentatively, for GME.
